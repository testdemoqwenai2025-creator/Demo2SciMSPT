Mathematics Modeling Analysis Domain: Math (M)

Abstract

This research short establishes the fundamental mathematical framework that underpins quantitative analysis across all domains of the SciMSPT interdisciplinary research platform. The mathematical foundations presented here provide the rigorous language and tools necessary for precise scientific description, prediction, and discovery.

Core Mathematical Disciplines

Linear Algebra & Matrix Theory:

  • Eigenvalue problems in quantum mechanics (Schrödinger equation solutions)
  • Singular value decomposition for dimensionality reduction in high-dimensional data
  • Tensor decompositions for representing multi-way relationships in materials science
  • Sparse matrix methods enabling large-scale computational physics simulations

Differential Equations:

  • Partial differential equations governing continuum mechanics, electromagnetism, and heat transfer
  • Stochastic differential equations for modeling systems with inherent randomness
  • Delay differential equations capturing temporal memory effects in physical systems
  • Numerical integration schemes preserving geometric structure (symplectic integrators)

Cross-Domain Relevance: The mathematical formalism presented here serves as the universal language connecting Science, Matter, Physics, and Time domains—enabling quantitative predictions that transcend traditional disciplinary boundaries.

Mathematical Methods for Scientific Computing

  1. Fourier & Wavelet Transforms: Decomposing signals into frequency components for spectral analysis, solving PDEs efficiently via pseudospectral methods, and multiscale representation of hierarchical data structures.
  2. Optimization Theory: Convex optimization for machine learning training, constrained optimization for materials design under multiple objectives, and global optimization navigating rugged energy landscapes.
  3. Probability & Statistics: Bayesian inference quantifying uncertainty in experimental measurements, Monte Carlo methods sampling high-dimensional configuration spaces, and extreme value theory predicting rare events.
  4. Differential Geometry: Manifold learning uncovering low-dimensional structure in complex datasets, Riemannian optimization respecting geometric constraints, and topological data analysis identifying persistent features.

Applications Across SciMSPT Domains

  • Science: Statistical hypothesis testing validating experimental results, information theory quantifying measurement content
  • Matter: Group theory classifying crystal symmetries, topology characterizing phases of matter
  • Physics: Functional calculus in quantum mechanics, gauge theories describing fundamental forces
  • Time: Dynamical systems theory analyzing temporal evolution, ergodic theory relating time averages to ensemble properties
