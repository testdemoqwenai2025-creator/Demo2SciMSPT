Statistical methods for time-dependent data analysis

Time Series Statistics Forecasting Domain: Time (T)

Abstract

This research short presents statistical and computational methodologies for analyzing sequentially ordered observations—time series data. Time series analysis techniques enable extraction of patterns, trends, and predictive insights from temporal data across scientific domains ranging from climate science to financial markets.

Fundamental Time Series Concepts

Stationarity & Non-stationarity:

  • Strict stationarity: Joint probability distribution invariant under time shifts
  • Weak stationarity: Constant mean, variance, and autocovariance depending only on lag
  • Trend-stationary vs. difference-stationary processes requiring different modeling approaches
  • Unit root tests (ADF, KPSS) distinguishing stochastic from deterministic trends

Decomposition Methods:

  • Additive decomposition: Yₜ = Tₜ + Sₜ + Rₜ separating trend, seasonal, and residual components
  • Multiplicative decomposition: Yₜ = Tₜ × Sₜ × Rₜ for seasonality proportional to level
  • STL (Seasonal-Trend decomposition using Loess) robust to outliers and missing values
  • Wavelet decomposition providing time-frequency localization for non-stationary signals

Prediction vs. Understanding: While machine learning approaches often prioritize prediction accuracy, classical time series methods emphasize interpretable parameters revealing underlying data-generating mechanisms—both perspectives are valuable within the SciMSPT framework depending on research objectives.

Modeling Approaches

  1. ARIMA Models: Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average models capturing autocorrelation structure through lagged values (AR), differencing for stationarity (I), and error smoothing (MA).
  2. State Space Models: Unobserved components framework enabling flexible specification of trend, seasonal, and cyclical dynamics with Kalman filtering for optimal state estimation.
  3. Machine Learning Methods: Recurrent neural networks (LSTM, GRU) learning complex temporal dependencies, transformer architectures with attention mechanisms capturing long-range correlations, and gradient boosting for feature-based forecasting.
  4. Bayesian Structural Time Series: Probabilistic frameworks quantifying uncertainty in forecasts, incorporating prior knowledge, and performing causal inference through intervention analysis.

Spectral & Frequency Domain Analysis

  • Fourier transform: Decomposing signals into sinusoidal frequency components identifying periodicities
  • Power spectral density: Distribution of signal power across frequencies distinguishing noise from structured variation
  • Coherence analysis: Frequency-domain correlation between two time series revealing common driving factors
  • Wavelet analysis: Multi-resolution representation adapting window length to local frequency content

Applications Across SciMSPT Domains

  • Climate science: Temperature trend detection, El Niño prediction, extreme event frequency analysis
  • Materials science: Degradation modeling, process control time series, sensor data monitoring
  • Finance: Volatility forecasting, risk modeling, algorithmic trading strategy development
  • Biology: Gene expression dynamics, circadian rhythm analysis, population ecology modeling
